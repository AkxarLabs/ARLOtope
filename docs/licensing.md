## Using the Lab

You can run the Lab for personal, academic or commercial research. Your existing project code,
papers, datasets, notes and ordinary research outputs do not become AGPL merely because you use
the Lab. Copying licensed software or assets into an output can carry separate obligations.

Reusable files under `templates/` retain **MIT** terms; generated projects include that notice.
This permits adapting the project scaffold without requiring an AGPL license for your research
code. Existing attached repositories keep their own licenses. Fonts, browser libraries and
vendored skills also retain their own notices; the default license does not replace them.

## Sharing or hosting a modified Lab

Keep copyright/license notices and identify your modifications. If you distribute covered
software, comply with AGPL's corresponding-source and licensing requirements. If you modify the
Lab and let users interact with it over a network, prominently offer those users the corresponding
source of your version, at no charge, as required by section 13. Internal network users count too.
You can charge for hosting or support while meeting these obligations.

The dashboard's **Settings → About & server** links to the upstream source. If you operate a
modified deployment, change the source link to your corresponding version and make all required
source available. An upstream link alone does not supply your unpublished changes. No source
archive is automatically published by the dashboard.

## Earlier releases and contributions

Material previously released under MIT keeps its existing permission grants. The original notice
is retained in `LICENSES/MIT-original.txt`. Future contributions use the license of the component
being changed; the default is AGPL-3.0-only. Keep upstream notices with vendored components.
This switch applies to this Lab repository; it does not relicense the separate Newtworks product.

Rewriting Git history or deleting and recreating the repository does not withdraw permissions
already granted for MIT-licensed copies. Public forks also survive deletion of the upstream
repository. Keep the history and original notices, and identify the license change clearly in the
new release. AGPL governs covered new work; it does not retroactively remove MIT permissions.
See GitHub's [repository deletion guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/creating-and-managing-repositories/deleting-a-repository)
for the effect on public forks, and the [MIT terms](https://choosealicense.com/licenses/mit/).
